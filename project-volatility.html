My Role: I served as the principal architect of the analytical framework and the primary interpreter of the econometric results. I designed the study's conceptual framework, analyzed the diagnostic test outputs, translated the Vector Autoregression (VAR) impulse responses, and interpreted the asymmetric leverage effects to explain how systemic risk propagates across the Philippine banking sector. The data extraction, statistical modeling, and generation of all high-fidelity plots and matrices were executed entirely in the R programming environment.

The Challenge

Classical financial theories often rely on the assumption that market risk—or variance—remains constant over time. However, decades of empirical evidence have proven this flawed. Financial time series consistently exhibit "volatility clustering," where calm periods are followed by calm periods, and turbulent periods are followed by violent market swings. This means market risk is highly dynamic, compounding upon itself during economic distress.

In the context of the Philippines, the Philippine Stock Exchange Composite Index (PSEi) is heavily weighted toward a few large-cap conglomerates and banking institutions (such as BDO, BPI, and Security Bank). We needed to map how volatility behaves and transmits between these apex stocks and the broader market to accurately assess systemic risk, especially surrounding exogenous shocks like the 2020 pandemic crash.

The Analytical Framework

We built a multi-stage, top-down econometric pipeline in R to systematically distill a decade of raw market data (December 2015 – December 2025) into actionable insights:

1. Data Sourcing & Stationarity

Extracted daily historical closing prices and transformed them into logarithmic returns to stabilize variance. We formally verified stationarity using Augmented Dickey-Fuller (ADF) testing.

$$r_t = \ln\left(\frac{P_t}{P_{t-1}}\right)$$

2. Univariate Analysis (Conditional Mean & Volatility)

We used ARIMA models to capture short-term linear predictability in the returns. After confirming the presence of ARCH effects, we modeled the conditional variance using asymmetric GARCH extensions (eGARCH, gjrGARCH) with Student's t-distributions to capture fat tails and leverage effects.

$$\sigma_t^2 = \omega + (\alpha + \gamma I_{\{\epsilon_{t-1}<0\}})\epsilon_{t-1}^2 + \beta\sigma_{t-1}^2$$

3. Multivariate Analysis (Dynamic Interrelationships)

We deployed Vector Autoregression (VAR) to examine directional influence and shock transmission speeds via Granger Causality and Impulse Response Functions (IRF).

$$Y_t = c + A_1 Y_{t-1} + A_2 Y_{t-2} + \dots + A_p Y_{t-p} + \epsilon_t$$

4. Time-Varying Correlations (DCC-GARCH)

Finally, we used the Dynamic Conditional Correlation (DCC-GARCH) model to capture how the structural relationships between the PSEi and its underlying banks evolve during periods of financial stress.

The Findings & Economic Impact

The empirical results entirely debunked the assumption of normal distributions and static risk in the Philippine market, yielding critical insights for portfolio managers and regulatory bodies:

Severe Asymmetric Leverage Effects: While the broader PSEi reacted symmetrically to news, individual banking stocks demonstrated highly significant, positive leverage effects. This proved that negative macroeconomic shocks trigger a significantly larger surge in volatility and panic selling in individual bank stocks compared to positive news of the exact same magnitude.

Information Hierarchy & Efficiency: Granger causality revealed that the PSEi, BDO, and Security Bank act as leading indicators, while BPI acts as a lagging receiver. Impulse Response Functions proved that while shocks transmit instantly, the Philippine market is highly efficient, absorbing disruptions and reverting to baseline equilibrium within 2 to 3 trading days.

The Failure of Diversification (Financial Contagion): The DCC-GARCH model proved that market correlations are not fixed. During the 2020 macroeconomic crisis, correlations that historically hovered between 0.20 and 0.50 violently spiked to above 0.70. This provided stark empirical evidence of financial contagion: during panic-driven phases, equities abandon their independent valuation metrics, lock together, and crash in tandem, rendering standard domestic portfolio diversification entirely ineffective during "black swan" events.
