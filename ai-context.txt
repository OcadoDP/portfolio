PROJECT 05: Two-Stage Offline Signature Verification
- Type: Machine Learning & Computer Vision (co-author, thesis approved November 2025; Best Thesis Presentation at a 2026 colloquium).
- Tech: Python, PyTorch, ResNet-18, Harris corner detection, ORB, KNN, Canberra distance; demonstration interface with Flask and React.
- Problem: Verify a static, scanned handwritten signature against multiple genuine references. No online pen-motion or writing-pressure data is used.
- Stage 1 / coarse filter: Detect Harris corners, compute ORB descriptors at those corners, form a normalized 256-bin descriptor histogram, use K-nearest-neighbor comparisons under Canberra distance, and apply a threshold derived from reference-signature distances. Candidate signatures rejected by Stage 1 do not proceed to Stage 2.
- Stage 2 / fine-grained verification: A Siamese CNN uses a ResNet-18 backbone INITIALIZED WITH RANDOM WEIGHTS, NOT an ImageNet-pretrained network. Training uses triplet loss. The network produces L2-normalized 128-dimensional embeddings. Verification compares the unknown signature against genuine references using cosine similarity, aggregates reference comparisons, and applies a threshold to accept or reject. Do not describe the decision as based only on the maximum reference similarity; the documented workflow uses reference-based average similarity.
- Results: Final reported accuracy 90.75%, Equal Error Rate (EER) 9.25%, Area Under the ROC Curve (AUC) 0.9649. These are final reported evaluation metrics, not averages over every dataset or guarantees in production.
- Data: Benchmark sources include CEDAR, GPDS, ICDAR, UTSig, and a locally collected set involving 60 participants.
- Scope: Offline image-based verification; not online dynamic biometric capture and not a one-image-pair-only comparison.
